Resources & support

Where to get help, report, and learn more.

If you’ve been affected by crime — or want to support someone who has — these are the places to start. Services vary by country, so always verify local numbers.

If you or someone else is in immediate danger

Call your local emergency number now — for example 999, 911 or 112. This page is for finding support, not for emergencies.

Find support

Help for what you’re going through.

Each of these services exists in most countries under a local name. Search for them in your area, or ask your local police or doctor to point you to the right one.

Victim support

Confidential advice and practical help — from reporting to recovery — for anyone affected by crime.

Domestic & family violence

Specialist support for people experiencing abuse at home, including safe housing and safety planning.

Mental health & wellbeing

Emotional support and counselling after trauma, distress or loss — including confidential helplines.

Cybercrime & online abuse

Help with hacking, image-based abuse, online harassment and fraud — including removal requests.

Fraud & scams

Where to report fraud, freeze compromised accounts, and get help recovering your money.

Youth & families

Support for young people and parents, including bullying, exploitation and online safety.

Report a concern

Who to tell, and when.

Reporting helps protect you and others. You can usually report to the police, and in many places also through a national online portal.

  • Emergency in progress — call your local emergency number
  • Non-emergency crime — your local police station or non-emergency line
  • Online fraud & scams — your national fraud or crime-reporting centre
  • Online abuse or image abuse — report to the platform, then to police
  • Hate crime — local police or a specialist reporting service
