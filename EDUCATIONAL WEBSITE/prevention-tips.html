Prevention tips

Small habits that reduce real risk.

You can’t remove every risk, but a few consistent habits make you — and the people around you — a much harder target.

!

In immediate danger?

Call your local emergency number now (for example 999, 911 or 112). Do not wait or rely on this page.

Risk-specific guides

Choose a topic to read practical prevention notes.

Open a card for clear, everyday steps that can help reduce risk. Prevention never guarantees safety or places blame on someone harmed.

01KidnappingIncluding mass abductions for ransom+

One of the most widespread and lucrative crimes, especially in the North-West and North-Central regions.

Prevention notes

Vary daily routines and travel routes. Avoid posting real-time locations or travel plans on social media. Travel in daylight when possible, use trusted transport, and share trip details with a reliable contact. In high-risk areas, strengthen community vigilance and early-warning networks with neighbours and local security.

02Armed robbery / Home invasionRisks at homes, businesses, and on the road+

Frequent in both urban and rural areas, often involving weapons and targeting homes, businesses, or people on the road.

Prevention notes

Secure homes with strong locks, good lighting, and, where possible, CCTV or alarm systems. Keep doors locked even for short periods, avoid displaying wealth, and do not open the door to unknown callers. When driving, keep doors locked, windows up in traffic, and avoid stopping in isolated spots.

03Banditry / Rural bandit attacksRisks affecting rural communities+

Armed groups raid villages, steal livestock, and attack communities, especially in parts of the North-West.

Prevention notes

Build strong community intelligence and early-warning systems. Cooperate closely with local security groups and agencies. Avoid predictable farming or market routines in high-risk zones, and support coordinated safety efforts such as joint patrols and information sharing.

04TheftPhone snatching, burglary, livestock and crop theft+

Very common at the everyday level; phone theft is widely reported, while livestock and crop theft can affect rural households hard.

Prevention notes

Keep phones and valuables out of sight in public. Use strong locks and lighting at home. For farmers, secure livestock at night and consider community watch systems. Avoid leaving items visible in vehicles and be extra alert in crowded markets or transport hubs.

05Cybercrime / Online fraud“Yahoo” and related scams+

Nigeria has a significant problem with internet-enabled fraud, identity theft, and romance or business scams.

Prevention notes

Never share OTPs, bank details, or personal information with strangers online. Enable two-factor authentication on important accounts. Verify payment or investment requests by calling the person on a known number. Be suspicious of too-good-to-be-true offers and report suspicious activity to the police or relevant agencies.

06Cultism / Gang-related violenceRival groups, territorial control, and violent clashes+

Particularly active in parts of the South and some urban centres, involving rival groups, territorial control, and violent clashes.

Prevention notes

Avoid association with cult groups and report recruitment pressure early. Stay away from known hotspots, especially at night. Parents and community leaders can promote positive youth programmes and early intervention. Schools and neighbourhoods can strengthen peer-support and reporting channels.
